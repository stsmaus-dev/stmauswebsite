7am. The gate opens.

Asked on a local Putty Facebook post, December 2023.

Do we just go in and say hi?

Yes.

Drive under the arch any day between 7am and 7pm. Park, walk up to the church or the bookshop, and ask for one of the Fathers. You do not need to book, and you do not need to be Coptic or Orthodox to be welcome here. Come as you are, and come quietly.

Two hours out of Sydney, the city is gone. The monastery has been on Putty Road since 1993, and monks have prayed here since 1995. Each day has the same shape: praise before dawn, the Liturgy through the morning, a meal, a talk with one of the Fathers, and quiet hours in the open air. You are welcome to share it.

Plan your visit

7am. Before you walk in.

A few kind requests.

For your own benefit and enjoyment during your visit, we ask only this.

  1. Keep your voice low. The quiet is why you came.
  2. Please dress modestly, as you would for church.
  3. Women are welcome all day. Overnight stays are for men only.
  4. The Fathers do not hear confessions. Please keep that for your parish priest. They are glad to sit and talk with you at 1pm.
  5. Bringing a group, or hoping to stay the night? Please call first on (02) 6579 7093. Youth groups need permission before an overnight stay.
  6. Ask the father in charge before you walk into the bush, visit the farm, or bring food or gifts for the monastery.
  7. Children are welcome. Please keep them close around the buildings and equipment.
  8. Follow the day's timetable, and leave the grounds as you found them.

1pm. A talk with a Father.

Monks you can talk to.

Monastic life began here in 1995, when two monks came from Egypt, Very Rev Fr Pavlos Anba Bishoy and Rev Fr Bishoy St Anthony. Australian-born monks followed them. Today H.G. Bishop Anba Daniel is Bishop and Abbot. The Fathers pray, work the land, publish books, and keep the gate open. At 1pm there is time to sit and talk with one of them.

Questions visitors ask, with plain answers.

The questions people ask.

  1. Do I need to book, or can I just turn up?

    For a day visit, just turn up. Day visitors are welcome from 7am to 7pm, every day. If you are coming as a group or by bus, please call first so the Fathers can expect you: (02) 6579 7093. If you hope to stay the night, call first too.

  2. I am not Coptic. Am I still welcome?

    Yes. You do not need to be Coptic, or Orthodox, to visit. Join the prayers, sit in on the 1pm talk, walk the grounds. Parish groups come every week, and visitors from other churches are welcome; the first Armenian clergy retreat was held here in 2017.

  3. Can women visit?

    Yes, for the day, and at every part of it. Overnight stays in the retreat house are for men only, so if you would like a longer stay, plan a full day instead, from the 9am Liturgy to departure at seven.

  4. Can we stay the night?

    Please call first on (02) 6579 7093. Overnight stays in the retreat house are for men, and youth need the monastery's permission before they stay. Overnight guests keep the full timetable, which begins with midnight praise at 4am.

    Retreat timetable and rules

  5. Can I confess to one of the monks?

    No. The monks do not hear confessions, so please speak with your parish priest. At 1pm each day there is a talk with H.G. Bishop Anba Daniel or one of the Fathers, and you are welcome to join it.

  6. Will I know what to do in the Liturgy?

    No one expects you to. Come in quietly, stand or sit with everyone else, and follow along. Ask a Father afterwards about anything you did not understand. If you would like to understand more before you come, the talks "The Hidden Joy of the Divine Liturgy" and "The Beauty of the Matins Prayers" and the playlist "Deep Dive into the Liturgy" are on the St Shenouda Press YouTube channel.

  7. Is there food?

    Yes. Lunch is at 12 and dinner at 4. If you would like to bring food or a gift, please ask the Father in charge first.

  8. How do we get there?

    8419 Putty Road, Putty NSW 2330. It is about two hours from Sydney by car, through Windsor and up Putty Road, through the bush between the Wollemi and Yengo national parks. It is 91 km from Windsor and 81 km from Singleton. There is no public transport. Coming with your parish? Some Sydney parishes run a bus. Ask at your church. One visitor's advice: "the road amazing to drive to it."

    Elia Rizgallah, Google review

  9. Can I bring the children?

    Yes. Church groups visit every week, and families come for the day. Please keep children close around the buildings and equipment, and visit the farm only with the Father in charge.

  10. Does it cost anything?

    There is no charge to visit. Donations are welcome.

  11. Is it a tourist attraction?

    No. It is a monastery, and visitors are welcome in it as guests of a place of prayer. Keep your voice down and keep to the timetable, and you will be very welcome.

  12. Is it wheelchair accessible?

    Please call and ask about what you need: (02) 6579 7093. We would rather talk it through than give you a wrong answer here.
